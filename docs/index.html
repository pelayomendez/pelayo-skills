01 · intro

A composable framework for AI-driven everyday work. Skills each do one job. Flows chain them into the loops you run every day. And when your loop isn't here, you write your own flow: one markdown file, no code.

Claude Code plugin · MIT · works with your tracker (Jira or similar) and forge (GitLab or GitHub)

Units → flows → your flows

UnitsOne job each. Check a claim with a fresh agent, review one MR, hand work to a worker, watch a pipeline, check a fix in a browser. They work alone too.
FlowsA daily loop, as a command. A flow only sets the order of its skills, where it stops for you, and what happens when a step fails.
Your flowsYour loop, your file. List the skills, write the steps, mark the stops. Or let /new-flow write it with you.

Bring your own skills

Some steps are slots: plug in the skill you already trust, and the flow keeps its stops and checks around it. Unset slots use ours.

# ~/.claude/slots.md
- implement: my-tdd-skill
- review: my-code-review-skill
- tests: my-test-review-skill

03 · flows

Flows that ship

A daily loop as a command. A flow only sets the order of its units, where it ⏸ stops for you, and what happens when a step fails.

Write your own flow

Type /new-flow and describe a loop you run by hand. It builds the flow with you and writes the file.

> /new-flow hotfix
ask    What starts it, and when is it done?
map    confirm the cause  → verify-claim
       fix it             → delegate (your implement slot)
       review             → review-change
       check in browser   → ui-check
       open the MR        missing → new unit, or a stop?
stops  merge: no grant in your charter → stop and ask
write  ~/.claude/skills/hotfix/SKILL.md
check  check-flow: ok · run it with /hotfix

Every step maps to a skill you already have. A step nothing covers becomes a small new skill or a stop, never hidden logic. Every push, merge or post gets a stop unless your charter grants it. Then check-flow reads the file before it ever runs.

The file it writes is plain markdown you can edit by hand:

---
name: hotfix
description: "One urgent bug, report to MR."
summary: "One urgent bug, report to MR"
disable-model-invocation: true
---

## Units
Steps, in order:
- verify-claim
- delegate
- review-change
- ui-check

## Steps
1. Confirm the cause with verify-claim.
2. delegate the fix to a worker.
3. review-change at the new head.
4. ui-check it locally.
5. Stop: show the MR, ask before merging.

Every skill below is a building block. The flows that ship are worked examples: open /support to see a full one, failures and all.

Checked before it runs

Each flow ships with a flow.yaml beside its text: its units, its slots, its stops and what each one guards. CI works out every effect a flow can have from its units, and fails if a push, merge, post or ticket change has neither a stop before it nor a grant in your charter. Flows from the community come in by pull request, labelled experimental until reviewed, and can't lean on your charter.

Install

/plugin marketplace add pelayomendez/yatsude
/plugin install yatsude@yatsude
/plugin install flow-map@yatsude   # optional: the yatsude panel

Or npx skills@latest add pelayomendez/yatsude to copy them into a project. Install one way, not both.

Needs: Claude Code; your tracker's CLI or MCP (for Jira, acli) and your forge's CLI (glab or gh) for the flows that touch tickets and MRs; a controlled-browser MCP (e.g. chrome-devtools) for browser checks. Details in the README.

FAQ

Does it push or merge without me? No. Merges, pushes and anything posted under your name wait for your word, unless your charter grants them.
GitHub or GitLab, Jira or something else? Skills name the step, not the tool; your flow config says which CLI or MCP to use.
How do I start? /backlog <epic> only reads. Then /new-flow to turn one of your own loops into a command.

The name is Japanese: yatsude (八つ手), "eight hands".
